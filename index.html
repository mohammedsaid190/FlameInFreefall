01 · The problem

Why a flame turns into a ball

On Earth, gravity shapes every flame. Take gravity away and fire follows different rules, so fire-safety rules learned on Earth don't always carry over.

On Earth 1 g

Hot gas is lighter than the air around it, so it rises. That rising plume pulls fresh air in from below and stretches the flame into a tall teardrop. Glowing soot makes it yellow.

vs

In microgravity ≈ 0 g

Nothing rises, so there's no plume. Oxygen only reaches the flame by slowly drifting in (diffusion) from every direction. The flame becomes a dim, round, blue sphere.

Why it matters now

The Moon will have richer air than Earth.

With Artemis, people are going back to the Moon and planning for Mars. Lunar habitats and pressurized rovers are expected to use air with 30% or more oxygen at lower pressure (≤ 10.2 psi), which is over 40% more oxygen than Earth's air. More oxygen means materials catch fire more easily and burn harder.

Lessons learned the hard way

60 years of fire in space

  1. 1967

    Apollo 1

    A cabin fire during a ground test killed three astronauts. The cabin held pure oxygen, which let the fire spread in seconds.

    Incident
  2. 1970

    Apollo 13

    An oxygen tank exploded on the way to the Moon. The crew survived, and it showed how dangerous oxygen systems can be.

    Incident
  3. 1997

    Mir station fire

    An oxygen generator burned for about 14 minutes with six people on board. What the crew learned shaped the design of the ISS.

    Incident
  4. 2007

    SAME

    The ISS tests how well smoke detectors notice smoke from overheated spacecraft materials.

    Detection
  5. 2009

    FLEX

    Burning fuel droplets reveal "cool flames" that keep burning after the visible flame is gone.

    Discovery
  6. 2016

    Saffire-I

    The largest fire ever lit on purpose in space burns inside an empty Cygnus cargo ship.

    Large scale
  7. 2017

    ACME

    Six gas-flame studies begin in the Combustion Integrated Rack, with oxygen up to 85%.

    Gas flames
  8. 2024

    Saffire-VI ends

    The last Saffire mission ends when its Cygnus burns up on re-entry, after nearly eight years of large-scale fire tests.

    Large scale

    05 · Bonus

    Space fire research, back on Earth

    What we learn in orbit also makes homes, cars and firefighters safer here on Earth.

    💧

    Water-mist extinguishers

    Fine mists put out fires using far less water. Understanding how flames die without gravity helps tune them.

    🚨

    Better smoke detectors

    SAME showed that many smoke particles are tiny and that some detectors miss them. That's useful for detecting fires early in buildings too.

    🧑‍🚒

    Firefighter gear

    Flame-resistant fabrics such as Nomex were tested in space fires and help us understand how protective materials hold up.

    ⚙️

    Cleaner engines

    The cool flames discovered in FLEX could help design engines that burn fuel more cleanly and efficiently.
